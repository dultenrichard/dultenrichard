Lessons from starting Bring Back Cadets

Dulten Richard · November 27, 2026 · AI-assisted editorial draft, prepared for publication; author review pending.

The early work of a youth advocacy initiative: public communication, evidence and follow-through.

In November 2026, I co-founded Bring Back Cadets in response to concerns about reductions in cadet training opportunities. The initiative set out to highlight the value of the program, share experiences and engage with the public and media.

A campaign begins with a problem, but a problem does not automatically produce a clear message. The work is to explain what is changing, why it matters and what information or action is being requested. A person's experience can explain the significance of an issue; it cannot by itself establish how widespread the issue is.

Collecting stories introduces another responsibility. Contributors should understand where their words may appear and whether their names or other details will be published. Private experiences should not become publicity material simply because they are relevant to a campaign.

On November 24, VOCM covered the announcement of additional funding and reported my response as a co-founder. A central question was when activities would resume. That distinction between an announcement and implementation is worth maintaining when describing the campaign's progress. Media coverage is not proof that one initiative alone caused a government decision.

Bring Back Cadets also includes a petition I created calling for improvements to sexual assault reporting procedures in Canadian cadet programs. It is a distinct issue within the wider initiative, and should be discussed without speculating about individual incidents or exposing private information.

At this early stage, the useful measures of progress include the accuracy of public statements, the quality of engagement, the clarity of requests and whether commitments are followed by documented action. An advocacy initiative should be willing to correct errors and distinguish what is known from what remains uncertain.

That is the standard I want this public record to reflect.
