02 Inquiry / The questions

Think
deeper.

Questions about power, place, and the institutions we inherit.

Political science, geography and research are not separate from the rest of life. They're ways of understanding the systems in which it unfolds.

2026Cities for People+

Research into whether pedestrian-oriented neighbourhoods, supported by public transit, provide better access to everyday needs and civic participation than car-oriented development.

The study begins with an ordinary observation: the distance between a home, a grocery store and a transit stop can shape daily opportunity.

Connected with Mapping global questions and A working philosophy.
2026Mapping global questions+

A geographical research atlas connecting human development, human rights concerns and major conflicts, with attention to the ways regional developments affect Canadian interests.

The objective is not to flatten complex societies into statistics, but to use evidence and maps as starting points for informed questions.

Connected with Cities for People and Questioning evidence.
2025Questioning evidence+

The R. v. Silip mock-trial experience involved preparing cross-examination questions, planning contingencies and delivering a closing statement.

Legal advocacy reinforced the value of distinguishing what someone knows from what they assume.

Connected with Mapping global questions.
The branch / I
03 Service / Responsibility

Earn
trust.

Responsibility begins before anyone gives you a title.

Cadet instruction, youth leadership, and voluntary service are places to practice what reflection alone cannot teach.

2026Earning trust in a new team+

A transfer to a new Royal Canadian Air Cadet squadron prompted an observation: experience travels with you; trust does not.

Effective leadership sometimes begins with listening, learning the routines that already exist, and contributing before seeking to direct.

Connected with The back row and A working philosophy.
2026Teaching through practice+

A leadership workshop for young athletes uses cooperative activities to explore resilience, compassion, accountability and teamwork.

The lesson is deliberately experiential: give people room to solve problems together and examine what they learn.

Connected with The back row.
2021–Learning to instruct+

The Air Cadet Program has provided opportunities to teach, organize and serve. The evolving record includes earlier experience with 661 RCACS and a later unit transition.

Earlier responsibilities remain in the archive without being portrayed as present appointments.

Connected with Earning trust and A working philosophy.

05 Field / Team

Play
your part.

Sport turns abstract qualities into immediate obligations.

Discipline, judgement, and team responsibility become tangible in the moments when effort and action count.

ReflectionThe back row+

Back-row rugby demands positioning, communication, repeated effort, and decisions under pressure. The experience offers a different language for understanding shared responsibility.

It connects directly to questions about leadership and instruction, rather than sitting alone as a sporting résumé entry.

Connected with Earning trust in a new team and Teaching through practice.

06 The person / The trunk

The
trunk.

Not a collection of titles, but a person shaped by what they choose to take seriously.

“To be a leader is to be liquid.”

OngoingA continuing biography+

A developing record of learning, service, sport, work, and a growing interest in political science and public life.

The archive is built to accommodate change in discipline, profession and perspective, without erasing what came first.

PrincipleA working philosophy+

“To be a leader is to be liquid” describes adaptability anchored by purpose. A commitment to leadership should be tested by the situations where it is practiced.

Connected with The back row and Earning trust.
