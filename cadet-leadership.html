What cadets taught me about responsibility

Dulten Richard · November 27, 2026 · AI-assisted editorial draft, prepared for publication; author review pending.

Lessons from cadet instruction, platoon leadership and mentoring younger cadets.

Joining Air Cadets in 2021 gave me a place to practise responsibility long before I had a senior appointment. The experience has since taken me from junior cadet training to instructing others and supporting summer training at Blackdown Cadet Training Centre.

In the summer of 2026, I spent two weeks in a platoon leadership role and four weeks supporting training. These were different jobs. One involved taking direct responsibility for a group; the other required helping staff and cadets across a course. Both depended on communicating expectations clearly and being available when someone needed assistance.

In a speech I prepared for course cadets, I told them: “If you ever encounter a situation with your staff and you want to talk about it, I'll always find the time for a chat.” The important part of that promise is following through. An invitation to speak means little if a person is dismissed when they actually approach you.

Standards and care belong together. A demanding course can still be a respectful environment. An instructor can correct a mistake without humiliating the learner, and can recognize when the same explanation is not working for everyone.

There is also a distinction between a leader's performance and the performance of the people they lead. A well-delivered lesson is not necessarily a successful lesson if nobody can apply what was taught. Checking understanding, asking questions and letting others practise are practical ways to find out whether instruction has worked.

The lesson I would carry into any future role is simple: responsibility means making yourself useful to the people who depend on you. The rank or title describes the appointment. What matters is how that responsibility is exercised.
