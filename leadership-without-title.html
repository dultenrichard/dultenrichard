Leadership without a formal title

Dulten Richard · November 27, 2026

A youth hockey workshop framework about communication, resilience and shared responsibility.

A captain's letter in hockey identifies a particular responsibility. It does not mean the rest of the team has no leadership role. That distinction was the starting point for a youth hockey leadership workshop lesson plan I developed in October 2026.

The workshop was designed for thirteen U13 players, with four themes: positive leadership, resilience, respect and compassion, and camaraderie. It was intended as a learning session rather than a captaincy tryout.

One planned activity put players in small groups to practise verbal communication on a simple marked walking path, with safety spotters and adult supervision. The exercise was meant to show how instructions can become more useful when a leader listens and adjusts instead of merely speaking louder.

Another activity asked groups to solve a cooperative problem. The goal was not to reward whichever player took control first, but to observe how the group included quieter teammates, responded to frustration and changed its approach. A short advertisement exercise would give each player a chance to contribute under unfamiliar conditions.

These activities can be followed by a short reflection: name one action another teammate took that helped the group. That question shifts attention from self-promotion toward observable behaviour. Encouraging a teammate after a mistake or making room for someone who has not spoken can be as important as issuing a direction.

The phrase I used in the workshop was “To be a leader is to be liquid.” By that I mean adapting to the person and the situation while keeping the team's standards intact. Sometimes a group needs someone to take charge. Sometimes it needs someone to listen.

A title can give you responsibility, but it cannot do the work for you.
