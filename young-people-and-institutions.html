What makes institutions accessible to young people?

Dulten Richard · November 27, 2026 · AI-assisted editorial draft, prepared for publication; author review pending.

Reflections on civic education, mock trial and practical youth participation.

In 2025, I participated in Rotary Adventure in Citizenship and competed in mock trial, where I received recognition for advocacy and a closing statement. Both experiences brought institutions into a setting where young people could ask questions, practise skills and participate rather than simply hear about them.

An institution can be familiar in name and still be difficult to understand in practice. Knowing that Parliament makes laws does not necessarily explain how a citizen can follow a debate, contact a representative or evaluate a proposed change. Knowing that a courtroom exists does not explain why evidence and procedure matter.

Mock trial offers one practical distinction: a convincing argument is not automatically a well-supported argument. A person must work from evidence, understand the rules of the exercise and respond to competing interpretations. That distinction matters beyond a classroom.

Programs such as Adventure in Citizenship can make public institutions more approachable by giving young people opportunities to encounter them directly. My involvement continued after the program through the 2025 alumni Instagram page, which provides a way for participants to remain connected.

For youth participation to be meaningful, there should be clear explanations of who makes decisions, how feedback is considered and what opportunities are available. Adults do not need to agree with every young person's opinion to take it seriously. They can explain the process, invite questions and give a reasoned response.

Youth engagement is not a single meeting or photograph. It is an ongoing relationship that benefits from reliable information, accessible processes and realistic expectations about what participation can achieve.
